## Q1 — How strong and from where are the afternoon breezes in the Maurienne and the Guisane; at what times do they make landings difficult?

### Takeaway
The Maurienne breeze is a classic powerful up-valley breeze. The Savoie CDVL describes it as "often strong", up to 50 km/h in summer. The Saint-Jean club tells pilots to land in the valley before 11:00 in summer. Haute-Maurienne sites (Aussois, Val Cenis) report a strong valley breeze from about 11:00, or earlier in a north wind. In the Briançonnais, the afternoon flow in the lower Guisane is mainly the Durance breeze pushing in from Briançon (>40 km/h at Briançon). It meets a NW / Lautaret flow descending the Guisane around Saint-Chaffrey. I found no explicit landing-time rule for Chantemerle.

### Cited Findings
**Maurienne (Aiguebelle → Saint-Jean → Saint-Michel → Modane)**
- The CDVL 73 technical notebook says Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne-area sites face mainly SE to SW, with 1000–1200 m height gain, takeoffs from 1500 m to over 2000 m in ski resorts, and a valley landing at Saint-Jean. In spring/summer pilots should either stay high to avoid the strong valley breeze or fly late afternoon to land in the valley. The valley breeze is "often strong (up to 50 km/h) in summer". The extract gives the landing altitude as 800 m, but the club gives 540 m (see next item). — [CDVL 73 – Cahier technique des principaux sites de Savoie](http://cdvl73.free.fr/cahier_technique_des_principaux_.htm)
- The local club (L'Envol de la Croix des Fleurs) gives this for the Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne landing: 540 m, oriented NW and SE (i.e. along the valley axis), access via N6 / rue Louis Sibué opposite the fire station. Glide ratio needed is 2.9 from Grand Châtelard and 2.5 from La Balme. Dangers: "attention vent de vallée l'été – se poser avant 11h" (paraphrased from extract). The landing is called technical because of railway-station works. — [L'Envol de la Croix des Fleurs – Les sites de vol](http://lenvoldelacroixdesfleurs.e-monsite.com/pages/les-sites-de-vol.html)
- Same club, takeoffs: **Grand Châtelard**, SW, 2000 m, reached from Jarrier (maison forestière de l'Oullaz, "Sur la Balme", 1 h walk), "vol uniquement le matin", watch the valley wind. **La Balme**, SE, 1580 m, takeoff towards the cross below the village, morning only, watch the valley wind; "si vent du nord, rester loin du Sappey". — [L'Envol de la Croix des Fleurs](http://lenvoldelacroixdesfleurs.e-monsite.com/pages/les-sites-de-vol.html)
- A general paragliding aerology article names the Maurienne (with the Arve, Tarentaise and Durance) among long Alpine valleys where the valley breeze can reach 50 km/h, or where a narrowing creates a venturi. It gives typical breeze speeds of 15–35 km/h in a layer 200–500 m or more thick, peaking around midday when both slopes are sunlit. If the breeze is too strong in flight, it advises landing above the breeze layer on a mountain slope. — [Rock The Outdoor – Brises de pente et brises de vallée](https://paragliding.rocktheoutdoor.com/media/les-brises-de-vallee/)

**Haute-Maurienne (Modane → Bonneval)**
- Aussois has 2 takeoffs (Angle Bellecôte; le Djoin) and 2 landings (la Charrière; la Croix). The extract reads « vent de vallée et thermique forts l'été à partir de 11h, plus tôt si vent du nord » (rendering of the extract). — [Maurienne Tourisme – Sites d'envol et d'atterrissage de parapente à Aussois](http://www.maurienne-tourisme.com/visiter_bouger/sites-d-envol-et-d-atterissage-de-parapente-a-aussois-5270645/); same POI on [Outdooractive](https://www.outdooractive.com/fr/poi/rhone-alpes/sites-d-envol-et-d-atterissage-de-parapente-a-aussois/67461529/)
- The Rock The Outdoor article says Aussois sits in a "relatively narrow valley that favours strong valley breezes starting from 11:30 in summer". — [Rock The Outdoor](https://paragliding.rocktheoutdoor.com/media/les-brises-de-vallee/)
- Val Cenis / Arcelles: the takeoff faces NE and W. Landing is in grassy fields upstream of Lanslevillard near the D902, close to the Antipodes school, and is private, so check with Val Cenis professionals first. Access is from Lanslevillard towards the Col du Mont-Cenis; turn left to Arcelles 500 m before the pass. The site gives good thermal restitution and fine evening flights. Valley breezes become strong from 11:00. "Méfiance avec la lombarde". — [FFVL – Terrain de pratique LANSLEVILLARD [14176]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/14176); [spots.guru – Arcelles Val Cenis](https://www.spots.guru/new_spots/paragliding-arcelles-val-cenis) (the extract merged both pages; which detail comes from which page is uncertain)

**Valloire / Galibier (Valloirette)**
- An FFVL sheet in the Valloire–Galibier sector gives: wind often strong at takeoff (experienced pilots); soaring possible "en restant au vent de la brise"; watch for venturi effects; ideal conditions "brise légère de Nord"; favourable sectors N, NE, NW; unfavourable E, SE, S, SW, W. Plan Lachat has a small hill used for soaring. — [FFVL – Terrain de pratique PLAN LACHAT GALIBIER [13680]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/13680). *Caveat: the extract also mentions the Crey du Quart takeoff, so these fields may come from the Crey du Quart sheet rather than Plan Lachat.*
- The Crey du Quart takeoff is at the top of the ridge, reached by the Valloire Galibier Thabor chairlift, at about 2500 m. It is used for ski-paragliding in winter, facing the Aiguilles d'Arves. — [Valloire tourisme – activités aériennes](https://www.valloire.net/en/profiter-de-valloire/activites-outdoor/dans-les-airs/); [Lenvair parapente](https://www.parapente-maurienne.com/)
- Valloire tandem flights take off from the Col du Galibier, with the meeting point on the D902 after "La Ferme du Galibier". — [CheckYeti – Valloire](https://www.checkyeti.com/fr/d/946/6763)

**Briançonnais / Guisane**
- Briançon is at the crossroads of 3 main valleys that channel the winds and have different breeze regimes. The Durance south of Briançon is open to S–SW wind and the up-valley breeze. The Guisane towards the Col du Lautaret channels NW winds well. — [Forum parabriancon – Aérologie du Briançonnais](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais)
- The Durance breeze rises from the south-west along the valley axis. The extract says it can be felt as far as "les Têtes de l'Argentière (2044 m)" (wording unclear in extract) and "at Briançon it can reach over 40 km/h". — [Chocard Airlines – La brise de la Durance](https://www.chocard-airlines.fr/topo-du-secteur/ph%C3%A9nom%C3%A8nes-locaux-et-pi%C3%A8ges/la-brise-de-la-durance)
- The convergence of the Durance breeze with the breeze and/or "petit nord-ouest" descending the Guisane is "very often around Saint-Chaffrey". — [Forum parabriancon – Aérologie du Briançonnais](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais); [Parapentiste.info – Nouveau à Briançon, infos sur sites](https://www.parapentiste.info/forum/sites-de-vols/nouveau-a-briancon-infos-sur-sites-t24833.0.html)
- FFVL Prorel – Croix de la Nore sheet (site 2359, run by the club Chocard Airlines; summer 1 July–30 Aug and winter 15 Dec–15 Apr lift seasons): ideal is "brises légères sans vent météo"; favourable sectors N and S, unfavourable NE and E; winter is ideal (1200 m height loss, thermals from mid-February); "au printemps et en été, les conditions peuvent être très fortes". Landing is at Saint-Blaise, the south exit of Briançon along the main road: a very large field aligned with the valley, with a high-voltage line across its north side. — [FFVL – BRIANCON - LE PROREL - CROIX DE LA NORE [2359]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/2359); [FFVL – SAINT-BLAISE landing [13227]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/13227)
- Serre-Chevalier: takeoffs at the top of the Forêt chairlift, the Vallons chairlift or the Prorel gondola, depending on the day's conditions. Landing is the gendarmerie car park in Saint-Chaffrey. — [Adrenactive – Baptême parapente Serre Chevalier](https://www.adrenactive.com/bapteme-parapente-serre-chevalier-8212.htm)
- A school's marketing page mentions "vents de vallée réguliers" and strong thermals in late spring and summer. It gives no figures. — [Axesse – Pourquoi voler à Serre Chevalier](https://www.axesse.com/pourquoi-voler-en-parapente-a-serre-chevalier/)

**Vallouise / Puy-Saint-Vincent**
- Puy-Aillaud (FFVL site 1124) is a morning site facing SE where the aerology "can start early, sometimes from 9:30, in all seasons". The site is surrounded by very high summits, so one flies "in aerology (slope and valley breezes) rather than meteo wind". When the breeze is strong, the west faces between Tête d'Amont and Condamine are in its lee, so fly high. A strong down-valley breeze in the evening needs watching. Landing is at the Chambonettes car park in Vallouise. The PSV takeoff is at the top of the Lauze chairlift, with landing at PSV 1400. — [Paraglidream – Sites de vol en Vallouise](https://www.paraglidream.com/sites-de-vol-en-vallouise); [FFVL – VALLOUISE - PUY AILLAUD [1124]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/1124)

### Inferences
- In the Maurienne, landing in the valley becomes difficult from about 11:00 local summer time. The club's advice ("land before 11h") and the Aussois/Val Cenis sheets ("strong from 11h") agree. The CDVL's alternative is to stay high until the breeze dies in the late afternoon. A north synoptic wind brings the breeze forward (Aussois).
- The Maurienne breeze flows up-valley from Aiguebelle to Saint-Jean (axis roughly N→S), then from Saint-Jean to Modane (W→E), then on to Lanslebourg–Bonneval (SW→NE). The Saint-Jean landing being oriented NW/SE matches the valley axis there.
- In the Guisane, the documented afternoon pattern is two opposing flows: the Durance breeze pushing NW up the lower Guisane from Briançon, and a flow descending from the Lautaret. The landings at Chantemerle/Saint-Chaffrey therefore sit close to the usual convergence and can see a shifting wind direction. This is my inference; no source says it directly.
- The Durance breeze >40 km/h at Briançon matters for the Saint-Blaise landing, which is aligned with the valley and so aligned with that breeze.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a time or speed for the Guisane breeze at Chantemerle/Villeneuve, or an explicit landing-time rule for Serre-Chevalier. The Chocard Airlines "phénomènes locaux et pièges" pages probably cover this but could not be fetched.
- No numeric speed for Haute-Maurienne (Aussois, Val Cenis say only "fort(e)"). No data at all for Bessans/Bonneval.
- No source on the evening/night down-valley regime in the Maurienne or the Guisane. Vallouise is the only sector with an explicit evening down-valley warning.
- No source for Clarée breezes.

## Q2 — How do Italian flows (Lombarde, east winds, Italian convergence) spill over the border passes and affect flying in Haute-Maurienne and Briançonnais?

### Takeaway
The Lombarde is an E–NE flow from the Po plain. It comes with high pressure over central Europe and a low south of Nice, and often with heavy precipitation on the Italian side. It spills over the border passes as a local foehn. At Briançon it descends from the Col de Montgenèvre, which is often capped by cloud. It is frequent in the afternoon, can reach about 20 km/h at the col or Les Alberts while still calm at Chalvet takeoff, and creates a convergence with the Durance breeze that starts at La Vachette and moves down the Durance. In Haute-Maurienne the Val Cenis sheets explicitly warn about the Lombarde, but no detailed mechanism was found.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: an easterly to north-easterly wind along the French–Italian border, blowing down from the high Alps out of Lombardy. Mostly gentle and dry on the Riviera, it can become violent and cold in winter, forming snowdrifts in mountain valleys. — [WeatherOnline – The Lombarde](https://www.weatheronline.pl/reports/wind/The-Lombarde.htm)
- Synoptic setting: high pressure with cold air over eastern France and central Europe, and a low of mild humid air south of Nice. — [AMS Glossary – Lombarde](https://glossarystaging.ametsoc.net/wiki/Lombarde)
- Météo-France forecaster Yan Giezendanner: Lombarde episodes hit the haute Tarentaise and **haute Maurienne** when heavy, localised precipitation piles up on the Italian side. This needs a Mediterranean low and a SE flow striking the chain. They are "completely natural", happen every year, sometimes several times a year, in winter and in summer. — [Lyon Capitale – Lombarde](https://www.lyoncapitale.fr/?p=120069)
- Prorel (Briançon) FFVL sheet: "Attention à la Lombarde (effet de foehn local) : le vent vient d'Italie et descend le col de Montgenèvre, souvent bouché". This is a rendering of the extract. In the original French the sheet likely reads close to this, but it is unverified. NE and E are listed as unfavourable sectors for the site. — [FFVL – Prorel Croix de la Nore [2359]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/2359)
- Montgenèvre: the Lombarde is described as an east wind from Italy that accelerates at the Col de Montgenèvre. It is frequent, especially in the afternoon, and can strengthen quickly and cause strong turbulence. It may not be felt at the Chalvet takeoff yet blow at 20 km/h at the col or at Les Alberts, which then becomes very turbulent. The Chalvet takeoff is at the top of the Chalvet chairlift, slightly to the west, in a wide marked zone run by Origin'Air. Landing is at the resort, preferably east of the obelisk to avoid the lines. — [Sev et Mika – Ski de rando et vol à Montgenèvre](https://sev-et-mika.fr/ski-rando-vol-parapente-montgenevre/) (the attribution of this extract to that blog page is probable, not certain; candidates in the same result set were [location-appartement-a-montgenevre.fr](https://location-appartement-a-montgenevre.fr/parapente-a-montgenevre/) and [Origin'Air](http://origin-air.com/bapteme-parapente-hiver-montgenevre/))
- Convergence between the Durance breeze and the "Lombarda": "begins around La Vachette, then gradually descends down the Durance valley". — [Forum parabriancon – Aérologie du Briançonnais](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais); [Parapentiste.info – Nouveau à Briançon](https://www.parapentiste.info/forum/sites-de-vols/nouveau-a-briancon-infos-sur-sites-t24833.0.html)
- Val Cenis / Arcelles (near the Col du Mont-Cenis): "méfiance avec la lombarde" (vent venant d'Italie). — [FFVL – Lanslevillard [14176]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/14176); [spots.guru – Arcelles](https://www.spots.guru/new_spots/paragliding-arcelles-val-cenis)

### Inferences
- The Lombarde hazard has a recognisable signature: a cloud cap ("col souvent bouché") on Montgenèvre or Mont-Cenis, with clear sky on the French side. Pilots should read it as the start of a downslope foehn flow into La Vachette–Briançon or Lanslebourg–Lanslevillard.
- In Haute-Maurienne the Lombarde descends from the Mont-Cenis (and probably the Bonneval-side border cols) towards the Arc. It opposes the up-valley Maurienne breeze, which flows SW→NE there. A convergence or shear somewhere around Lanslebourg–Termignon is physically likely, but **no source documents it**.
- Because the Lombarde is described as frequent in the afternoon at Montgenèvre, a morning that is calm on the Briançon side does not rule it out later in the day.

### Gaps
- No source found on the "convergence italienne" (the Po-plain breeze pushing over the main ridge on summer afternoons) as distinct from the synoptic Lombarde. Nor on its effect on Bonneval/Bessans or the Clarée.
- No figures for Lombarde strength at Briançon beyond the "20 km/h at the col/Les Alberts" example. No guidance on thresholds (e.g. pressure gradient Turin–Grenoble) that local pilots use.
- The Météo-France explanation was found only second-hand (press article). The Météo-France page itself was not reachable.

## Q3 — Where are the documented convergence lines?

### Takeaway
Three convergences are documented for the Briançonnais, all from local club and forum sources:
- **Saint-Chaffrey (lower Guisane)**: the Durance breeze against the breeze and/or small NW descending the Guisane via the Lautaret. Seen from the Granon, this is the confluence you fly in during summer.
- **Briançon ↔ verrou north of L'Argentière**: the southerly Durance breeze against NW–N synoptic wind. Over Briançon when the wind is weak, further south (often at the glacial verrou north of L'Argentière) when it is stronger.
- **La Vachette → down the Durance**: the Durance breeze against the Lombarde.

No convergence line was documented for the Maurienne, Valloire/Galibier or Vallouise in the sources reachable.

### Cited Findings
- "Convergence entre la brise remontant la Durance et la brise (et/ou petit nord-ouest descendant la Guisane) : très souvent située vers Saint-Chaffrey". This is a rendering of the extract. — [Forum parabriancon – Aérologie du Briançonnais](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais); [Parapentiste.info – Nouveau à Briançon](https://www.parapentiste.info/forum/sites-de-vols/nouveau-a-briancon-infos-sur-sites-t24833.0.html)
- "In summer at the Granon … you fly in breeze regime, in a confluence between the breeze rising up the Durance and the one coming via the Lautaret. It's somewhat special (turbulence risk) but can fly very well." — [Parapentiste.info – Nouveau à Briançon](https://www.parapentiste.info/forum/sites-de-vols/nouveau-a-briancon-infos-sur-sites-t24833.0.html)
- "The confluence zone between the southerly breeze and NW to N meteo wind lies either over Briançon itself if the wind is weak, or further south if the meteo wind is a bit stronger, often towards the glacial verrou north of L'Argentière." — [Chocard Airlines – La brise de la Durance](https://www.chocard-airlines.fr/topo-du-secteur/ph%C3%A9nom%C3%A8nes-locaux-et-pi%C3%A8ges/la-brise-de-la-durance) and/or [Forum parabriancon](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais) (the search extract did not separate the two)
- Durance breeze against the Lombarde: begins around La Vachette, then gradually moves down the Durance. — [Forum parabriancon](https://parabriancon.forumactif.org/t228-aerologie-du-brianconnais)
- The Col du Lautaret (2058 m) is the boundary between the Romanche and Guisane valleys. The road over it is periodically closed for very strong wind. One extract calls it a kite-boarding "mecca" that "catches the winds from the east and west". — [Wikipedia – Col du Lautaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_du_Lautaret); [France 3 – col du Lautaret fermé (vent fort)](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/auvergne-rhone-alpes/isere/vent-fort-col-du-lautaret-ferme-circulation-deviation-mise-place-1792255.html); [Alpes 1 – Lautaret fermé, vent très fort](https://alpesdusud.alpes1.com/news/hautes-alpes/111278/le-col-du-lautaret-ferme-a-cause-d-un-vent-tres-fort); [PisteHors – Col du Lautaret](https://pistehors.com/backcountry/wiki/Hautes-Alpes/Col-Du-Lautaret) (attribution of the kite remark probable)

### Inferences
- The Saint-Chaffrey convergence and the Granon confluence are the same feature seen from the valley floor and from the north ridge. The JSON draws it as one line across the Guisane, Granon → Chantemerle/Saint-Chaffrey → south slope.
- The Briançon/L'Argentière convergence slides along the Durance with the strength of the N–NW synoptic wind. The JSON draws it as a segment Briançon → Prelles → verrou north of L'Argentière, labelled "position glissante".
- The Lautaret is clearly a wind gate between the Oisans and the Guisane, and the "flow coming via the Lautaret" in the Granon confluence implies a pass transfer from the Romanche side. No paragliding source in reach described a "convergence du Lautaret" at the col itself.

### Gaps
- The "convergence at Col du Lautaret" and "convergence at Briançon" mentioned in the assignment were confirmed only as described above. I found no source for a Galibier convergence (Valloirette N breeze against Guisane/Lautaret flow), even though the Valloire sheet shows a N breeze at Plan Lachat and the Granon sources show a flow descending from the Lautaret.
- No documented convergences for the Maurienne (e.g. Arvan/Arc confluence at Saint-Jean, Valloirette/Arc at Saint-Michel), Haute-Maurienne (Lombarde against Arc breeze) or Vallouise (Gyronde/Durance at L'Argentière).

## Complementary objective — sites, venturis, synoptic effects and XC routes by sub-sector

### Takeaway
The key traps that are documented:
- Maurienne breeze up to 50 km/h, with the Saint-Jean landing before 11:00.
- Haute-Maurienne breeze strong from 11:00.
- Venturi and strong takeoff wind on the Valloire–Galibier side in the N breeze.
- The Lautaret as a wind gate.
- The Lombarde at Montgenèvre/Mont-Cenis.
- The lee of the breeze on the Vallouise west faces, plus the strong evening down-valley breeze there.
- Very strong spring/summer conditions at the Prorel.

Classic XC routes (Briançon–Galibier–Maurienne, Serre-Chevalier–Écrins, Durance line) could not be documented with any reachable source.

### Cited Findings
- Sites around Briançon are chosen by the day's wind: Col du Granon, Col du Galibier, Vallouise, Puy Chalvin (Emotion'Air listing). — [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur Tourisme – Parapente Briançon](https://provence-alpes-cotedazur.com/de/offres/parapente-briancon-de-2861275/)
- Takeoff list for Briançon–Serre-Chevalier: Le Granon, Le Petit Aréa, L'Yret, Sommet de Serre-Chevalier, Le Prorel, Le Grand Aréa, L'Ombilic – Les Gondrands, Croix de la Nore, Les Tronchets (Villar-Saint-Pancrace). — [Barbules Wiki – Site:Serre-Chevalier](http://barbules.fr/index.php/Site:Serre-Chevalier)
- Prorel: takeoff and landing are forbidden on ski slopes. Keep at least 50 m lateral and 15 m vertical distance from all lift installations. — [FFVL – Prorel [2359]](https://federation.ffvl.fr/terrain/2359)
- Effect of north wind: Aussois breeze earlier; La Balme, stay away from Le Sappey; Valloire–Galibier sheet favours N/NE/NW; Briançon convergence pushed south towards L'Argentière. — sources as cited in Q1 and Q3.
- General: in a breeze that is too strong, land above the breeze layer on a slope. — [Rock The Outdoor](https://paragliding.rocktheoutdoor.com/media/les-brises-de-vallee/)

### Inferences
- In the Valloirette, an up-valley breeze from the N (Saint-Michel → Télégraphe → Valloire → Galibier) is consistent with the Plan Lachat/Crey du Quart sheet's "brise légère de Nord" and its N/NE/NW favourable sectors. It probably pushes air over the Galibier towards the Lautaret–Guisane in the afternoon. That last part is my inference, not a source finding.
- The upper Durance between Embrun and Briançon carries a single long up-valley breeze (SW axis). Pilots crossing from Briançon towards Embrun fly into it, and in N–NW synoptic wind they meet the convergence somewhere between Briançon and the verrou above L'Argentière.

### Gaps
- **XC routes**: no source reached for Briançon–Galibier–Maurienne, Serre-Chevalier–Écrins, or Briançon–Embrun routes, transitions or typical distances. XC Mag guides, CDVL 05/Ligue PACA cross pages and XContest could not be fetched. The JSON leaves `xc_routes` empty rather than inventing.
- **Thermal triggers**: no documented named trigger points (barres, éperons) for any sub-sector, apart from the SE morning slope at Puy-Aillaud.
- **Sybelles / Arves / Thabor**: no aerology source found (Le Corbier, La Toussuire, Saint-Sorlin, Albiez, Valmeinier/Thabor).
- **Bonneval/Bessans, Clarée/Névache, Pelvoux/Ailefroide, L'Argentière takeoffs**: no site sheet found.
- **W, SW and Foehn (northern foehn / S flow)**: apart from "Durance open to S–SW" and the Prorel favourable S sector, nothing specific.
- **Coordinates**: none verified against an authoritative page (all fetches blocked). Every point in the JSON is `approx`. The takeoffs in particular (Croix de la Nore, Crey du Quart, Arcelles, Chalvet, Puy-Aillaud, Grand Châtelard, La Balme, Serre-Chevalier) should be corrected from the FFVL sheets (n° 2359, 13227, 13680, 14176, 1124, …) before production use.
