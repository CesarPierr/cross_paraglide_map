## Q1: What do Chamonix sources say about valley breeze strength and timing (Bois du Bouchet, etc.), and how does glacier/katabatic flow interact?

### Takeaway
The official Chamonix landing sheet says the Arve/Chamonix up-valley breeze is "généralement soutenue l'après-midi (entre 20 et 30 km/h)". It warns pilots to watch for the breeze strengthening at the start of the afternoon. The Pays du Mont-Blanc brochure gives "20-25 km/h usually, up to 30 km/h". The high takeoffs (Planpraz, Flégère) are morning sites, and Plan de l'Aiguille is the classic evening "restitution" site. I found no source on glacier katabatic winds.

### Cited Findings
- **Bois du Bouchet (official Chamonix landing, 1,040–1,046 m)**: the valley breeze is generally sustained in the afternoon, between 20 and 30 km/h. Watch for the breeze strengthening at the beginning of the afternoon. FR wording reconstructed from the snippet: "la brise de vallée est généralement soutenue l'après-midi (entre 20 et 30 km/h)… attention au renforcement de la brise de vallée en début d'après-midi". — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Terrain d'atterrissage du Bois du Bouchet](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/terrain-datterrissage-du-bois-du-bouchet-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884696/); same text on [Combloux OT](https://www.combloux.com/en/equipment/landing-ground-of-the-bois-du-bouchet/) and [Passy OT](https://www.passy-mont-blanc.com/en/?p=12037)
- Bois du Bouchet approach: the field is bordered by trees. Approach it while staying upwind of the trees, especially those along the small access road to the gravel pit. The landing cannot be seen from the takeoff, so locate it before going up. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/terrain-datterrissage-du-bois-du-bouchet-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884696/); [Passy OT, Bois du Bouchet](https://www.passy-mont-blanc.com/en/equipement/terrain-datterrissage-du-bois-du-bouchet/)
- Bois du Bouchet GPS: N45°55'53" E006°52'43" (→ 45.9314 N, 6.8786 E), altitude 1,040 m. It serves the Planpraz, Plan de l'Aiguille, Flégère, Grands Montets and Aiguille du Midi takeoffs. — [Free flying in the Mont Blanc Region (Chamonix OT brochure, PDF)](https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/vol-libre-anglais.pdf); [Passy OT](https://www.passy-mont-blanc.com/en/equipement/terrain-datterrissage-du-bois-du-bouchet/)
- Winter: a municipal by-law bans the Bouchet and Savoy landings while the alpine and cross-country ski runs are open. Pilots must then land on the hang-glider field 500 m upstream (north). That emergency field is described as "aérologiquement malsain et dangereux" (aerologically unhealthy and dangerous). — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/terrain-datterrissage-du-bois-du-bouchet-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884696/); [Chamonix.net, atterrissage interdit au Bois du Bouchet](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/actualite/chamonix-atterrissage-interdit-parapente-au-bois-du-bouchet)
- Brochure: "thermic valley winds can be as strong as 30 kph but are more often between 20-25 kph". It refers to "the valley wind of the Arve valley" and says some landing areas are upwind and hard to reach when the wind is strong. — [Free flying in the Mont Blanc Region (PDF)](https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/vol-libre-anglais.pdf) (summary-level; FR version: [airsportschamonix.fr PDF](https://www.airsportschamonix.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/vol_libre_chamonix_Mont-Blanc_fr.pdf), [FFVL PDF](https://federation.ffvl.fr/sites/ffvl.fr/files/Massifdumontblancchamonix.pdf))
- **Planpraz (Brévent)**: 2,000–2,032 m, faces S and NE, GPS N45°56'08" E006°51'18" (→ 45.9356 N, 6.8550 E), 1,000 m vertical drop. It is "intéressant de 8h jusqu'en début d'après-midi" (interesting from 8 am until early afternoon). Hazards: the SW cables of the Brévent cable car in front of the takeoff exit (move well to the right to pass under them), and turbulence in W and N winds above 20 km/h at 2,000 m. There is an FFVL beacon at the Brévent summit, 500 m above the takeoff. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Décollage de Planpraz-Brévent](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-de-planpraz-brevent-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5885016/); [Free flying in the Mont Blanc Region (PDF)](https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/vol-libre-anglais.pdf)
- **La Flégère**: GPS N45°58'06.6" E006°52'33.4" (→ 45.9685 N, 6.8759 E), faces S to E, 2,350 m, 1,350 m drop. Best "de 8h à la fin de matinée" (8 am to the end of the morning). Not usable in winter. Watch the cable-car cables to the right of the takeoff. — [Chamonix.net, La Flégère](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/loisirs/parapente/la-flegere)
- **Plan de l'Aiguille**: 2,231 m, faces W and NE. Pilots launch facing west, which is ideal for evening flights. The NE side is used in north wind. Conditions: thermal and thermodynamic breezes, or a light westerly flow. In thermal conditions "good experience is necessary, as the breeze can be sustained at takeoff". Hazards: cable-car power cables on the left and cable-car cables on the right when leaving the takeoff. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Décollage du Plan de l'Aiguille](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-du-plan-de-laiguille-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884743/); [Combloux OT](https://www.combloux.com/en/equipment/needle-plane-takeoff/)
- Plan de l'Aiguille is "known locally for its huge evening flights". Evening flights along the Mont-Blanc massif can cover 20–30 km, and "in the space of 2 hours it is possible to traverse 11 glaciers to the end of the Chamonix valley". — [Chamonix.net, Plan de l'Aiguille (EN)](https://www.chamonix.net/english/leisure/paragliding/plan-aiguille); [nomadness.benlo.com](https://nomadness.benlo.com/chamonix.html) (the search summary blended both; exact attribution uncertain)
- **Grands Montets**: takeoff around 3,100 m with a 2,100 m drop, on the Pas de Chèvre glacier facing W toward Chamonix. Crevasses open in summer and widen a lot in August–September. Morning starts allow long XC. In the best conditions "a cloud base at 4,000 m" lets pilots get high over the Drus, Aiguille Verte and Chardonnet. Evening "restitution" flights are possible over the Pas de Chèvre and Becs Rouges. — [Chamonix.net, Grands Montets](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/loisirs/parapente/grands-montets)
- **Le Prarion (Les Houches)**, NE/W takeoffs at about 1,853–1,945 m: a morning site "avec thermiques d'est ou flux descendant la vallée (bise ou léger foehn)" (east thermals, or flow coming down the valley in bise or light foehn). "Après 11h, l'atterrissage devient aléatoire et est à éviter l'après-midi par brise quasi quotidienne" (after 11 am the landing becomes unreliable; avoid it in the afternoon because of the almost daily breeze). The landing at Les Chavants lies downwind of a relief once the valley breeze sets in, and there is turbulence at the end of the takeoff. FR wording reconstructed. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Décollage du Prarion](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-du-prarion-les-houches-fr-5884871/)
- Brochure GPS for Prarion NE: "N 45° 53' 46" - E 006°44'62"", NE-facing, 1,853 m, 843 m drop. The longitude seconds (62") are invalid as printed. — [Vol libre au Pays du Mont-Blanc (FFVL PDF)](https://federation.ffvl.fr/sites/ffvl.fr/files/Massifdumontblancchamonix.pdf)
- La Chamoniarde: Grand Prarion is flyable only "if the breeze is directly on", which generally happens at the beginning or end of the afternoon. — [La Chamoniarde, Free flying](https://www.chamoniarde.com/en/your-activities/free-flying)
- Brochure snippet: you can fly over Servoz to the Chedde landing, finding thermals on the Servoz and Plaine-Joux side of the valley. — [Free flying in the Mont Blanc Region (PDF)](https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/vol-libre-anglais.pdf)
- FFVL/Pays du Mont-Blanc PDF snippet: one site has the "brise montante de vallée en fin d'après-midi". "Avec un régime de NE, les brises diurnes priment sur le vent météo, protégeant le site" (in a NE regime the daytime breezes dominate the weather wind and protect the site). The summary attributed this to Plaine-Joux, but quoted Planpraz's GPS, orientation and altitude, so the attribution is **unreliable**. — [FFVL PDF](https://federation.ffvl.fr/sites/ffvl.fr/files/Massifdumontblancchamonix.pdf); [Haute-Savoie Tourisme, Plaine-Joux](https://www.haute-savoie-tourisme.org/loisirs/lieux-sportifs/sites-d-envol-et-d-atterissage/124385-aire-de-decollage-de-plaine-joux)
- Plaine-Joux (Passy) is a drive-up launch with "generally mellower conditions than in the valley itself". It works "from midday until late". — [SeeChamonix paragliding guide](https://www.seechamonix.com/paragliding/guide) / [XC Mag Guide to Chamonix](https://xcmag.com/travel-guide/guide-to-chamonix-france/) (attribution between the two uncertain)
- Barbules wiki (Chamonix – Vallée du Mont-Blanc): Passy Plaine-Joux is favourable "en brises de pente". Some sites "experience very strong valley winds in the afternoon, with aerological conditions sometimes being violent". — [Barbules wiki, Site Chamonix](http://barbules.fr/index.php/Site:Chamonix_-_Vall%C3%A9e_du_Mont_Blanc)

### Inferences
- Timing pattern in the Chamonix valley: morning slots on the S-to-E-facing Aiguilles Rouges launches (Flégère and Planpraz, from 08:00 to late morning or early afternoon). The breeze strengthens sharply from early afternoon, typically 20–30 km/h at Bois du Bouchet. The Plan de l'Aiguille W face is the evening option. Landing at Les Houches (Les Chavants) becomes a lee landing once the breeze is in, after about 11:00. This is consistent across the four official sheets.
- The breeze flows up-valley from Servoz and Les Houches toward Chamonix and Argentière. This direction is an inference from valley geometry and from the "500 m upstream (north)" wording about the Bouchet emergency field; no source states it as a compass direction.
- Prarion's NE takeoff faces the Chamonix valley and gets morning E thermals or down-valley flow. Its W takeoff ("Grand Prarion") seems to need the afternoon breeze blowing straight in, presumably the Arve/Saint-Gervais-side breeze. This is my reading of the Chamoniarde wording, not stated in the sources.

### Gaps
- **Glacier/katabatic winds**: I found no source on glacier winds (Bossons, Taconnaz, Mer de Glace, Argentière) or on how they interact with the valley breeze or evening restitution. This is not answered.
- No source gives the breeze direction or strength in the Servoz/Arve gorge (Servoz venturi) or at Col des Montets/Vallorcine.
- I found no Chamonix school wording (e.g., Les Ailes du Mont-Blanc, Summits, Air Sports Chamonix) beyond the tourism-office/FFVL sheets.

## Q2: Where are the convergences, and what are the airspace and regulation constraints (Mont-Blanc summit zone, Aiguille du Midi cables)?

### Takeaway
From 1 July to 31 August, a ministerial order of 13 October 2008 (zones LF-R 30 A/B) forbids flying over the core Mont-Blanc massif, including Plan de l'Aiguille, the Aiguille du Midi and the Vallée Blanche. Violations risk criminal prosecution, fines and confiscation of equipment. Later changes added ZRT 30 C (H24, 1 June–15 October) and new LF-R 30 B limits (2021 SUP AIP). The Aiguilles Rouges reserve imposes a 1,000 m/ground minimum. I found **no source** describing convergence lines (Col de Voza, Megève, Saisies, Petit-Saint-Bernard).

### Cited Findings
- A restricted zone has existed on the French side since October 2008. It bans flying in the red zones from 1 July to 31 August every year. Anyone who overflies it, voluntarily or involuntarily, risks criminal prosecution, fines and confiscation of their flying equipment. — [XC Mag, Mont Blanc restricted zones](https://xcmag.com/?p=44321); [Chamonix.net 2012-08-20](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/actualite/2012-08-20.htm)
- "Du 1er juillet au 31 août, le survol du massif du Mont-Blanc est réglementé par l'arrêté du 13 octobre 2008" (from 1 July to 31 August, flying over the Mont-Blanc massif is regulated by the order of 13 October 2008). Many paragliders have been seen in the summer no-fly zone for ultralights (PUL), including the Vallée Blanche, Plan de l'Aiguille and Aiguille du Moine. — [Chamonix.net 2012-08-20](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/actualite/2012-08-20.htm)
- Plan de l'Aiguille: takeoff and overflight are forbidden in July and August by ministerial decree. The Aiguille du Midi is closed July–August, and the Vallée Blanche (south takeoff) has restrictions. One source gives helicopter traffic as the reason. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Plan de l'Aiguille](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-du-plan-de-laiguille-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884743/); [Chamonix.net, Aiguille du Midi](https://www.chamonix.net/english/leisure/paragliding/aiguille-du-midi)
- Pilots must follow general aviation rules plus the Mont-Blanc-specific ones (ministerial decree LF-R 30 A and B). After the "Zone de protection des habitats naturels du Mont-Blanc – site exceptionnel" (APPHN) was created, LF-R 30 A/B were modified and a ZRT 30 C was added, active H24 from 1 June to 15 October. — [La Chamoniarde, Free flying](https://www.chamoniarde.com/en/your-activities/free-flying); APPHN background: [DREAL AURA, avis CSRPN dérogation APPHN Mont-Blanc 2022 (PDF)](https://www.auvergne-rhone-alpes.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/020_avis_csrpn_derogation_apphn_mont_blanc_ddt74_20220505.pdf)
- 2021 SUP AIP: LF-R 30 B has modified lower limits and new lateral limits on either side of LF-R 30 A, between "lac des Gaillands" and "pointe de Gigord", from 1 July to 31 August. — [AeroVFR, un SUP AIP pour le secteur du Mont-Blanc](https://aerovfr.com/2021/05/un-sup-aip-pour-le-secteur-du-mont-blanc)
- Free flying in the Chamonix valley is restricted from 1 July to 31 August by ministerial decree. The authorised landings are Bois du Bouchet (main), Savoy (closed in winter) and the "Cachat gravel pit". Approved third-party liability insurance and a helmet are compulsory. — [La Chamoniarde, Free flying](https://www.chamoniarde.com/en/your-activities/free-flying)
- Planpraz: beyond the summit ridge to the W/NW, flying lower than 1,000 m above ground is forbidden over the Aiguilles-Rouges nature reserve. — [Chamonix OT, Take off from Planpraz-Brévent](https://en.chamonix.com/activities/summer/paragliding/take-off-from-planpraz-brevent)
- Cable hazards: Brévent cable car SW of Planpraz, Flégère cables right of the takeoff, and Aiguille du Midi cable-car power cables left and right of Plan de l'Aiguille (see Q1 sources).

### Inferences
- The July–August ban removes Plan de l'Aiguille and the Mont-Blanc face from summer XC planning. Summer flying in the valley then relies on the Aiguilles Rouges side (Planpraz, Flégère, Brévent) and on Prarion/Plaine-Joux. The red-zone limits ("lac des Gaillands", "pointe de Gigord") suggest the zone's western/valley edge runs along the Chamonix valley floor at Les Gaillands. This needs confirming on the SIA map.

### Gaps
- No source found on convergences at Col de Voza, the Megève saddle, Col des Saisies or Col du Petit-Saint-Bernard. The JSON convergences are flagged low confidence and "déduction".
- No exact LF-R 30 A/B or ZRT 30 C polygons or altitudes were obtained (SIA/AIP pages were not reachable).
- The Aiguille du Midi–Helbronner cable (Panoramic Mont-Blanc) and its NOTAM/charted status were not found.

## Q3: How does the Tarentaise breeze behave, and how do the side valleys feed it (Bozel, Belleville, Peisey, Beaufort; Les Arcs, La Plagne, Courchevel, Méribel, Pralognan, Val d'Isère)?

### Takeaway
The only local primary source found is the Parapente Club Haute Tarentaise (PCHT). Its aerology pages say the main Isère breeze "se divise à Landry pour alimenter la vallée de Peisey-Nancroix". The Peisey-Vallandry slope then gets the best afternoon conditions. Schools describe the breeze becoming quite strong by mid-afternoon. General references put Tarentaise-type valley winds up to 50 km/h with venturi effects. Nothing specific was found for the Doron de Bozel, Belleville or Beaufort.

### Cited Findings
- "The main valley breeze divides at Landry to feed the Peisey-Nancroix valley and provides the best afternoon conditions on the Peisey-Vallandry slope." Reconstructed FR: "la brise principale se divise à Landry pour alimenter la vallée de Peisey-Nancroix…". — [PCHT, Aérologie Hte Tarentaise](https://pcht.org/articles.php?lng=fr&pg=977&mnuid=426&tconfig=0) (the search summary did not name the page explicitly. It is the only aerology page in that result set, so attribution is likely but not certain.)
- PCHT was founded on 26 March 1986. It manages flying sites "de Ste Foy Tarentaise à La Plagne, du Fort de la Platte au Domaine des Arcs". Its downloads include "Aerologie-de-la-Tarentaise.pdf", and it lists wind beacons via winds.mobi. It also has a page for the "Atterrissage des Ilettes (Hauteville)". — [PCHT home](https://www.pcht.org/); [PCHT Télécharger](https://www.pcht.org/download.php); [PCHT, Atterrissage des Ilettes](https://pcht.org/decos_atterros/atterro_ilettes.php)
- The breeze "becomes quite strong in mid-afternoon". Summer afternoons are more turbulent and are kept for "Sensation" tandem flights. — school page [Là-Haut Parapente, Albertville](https://www.lahautparapente.com/vol-et-bapteme-en-parapente-alberville) (attribution between the result-set pages is uncertain)
- General: in long Alpine valleys "such as the Tarentaise, Maurienne, Durance", valley-breeze speeds can reach 50 km/h, partly through venturi where the valley narrows. The up-valley breeze is strongest from 14:00 to 16:00–17:00. — [Wikipedia FR, Régime de brise](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9gime_de_brise); [P.-P. Ménégoz, Les brises (PDF)](https://www.eurenciel-mjc.fr/upload/documents/bfb0a2ea1fe045ff93a56a009926d8da.pdf) (pedagogical sources, not site-specific; attribution between them is uncertain)
- **Les Arcs**: official seasonal takeoffs include Vezaille (Arc 1600) and the solo takeoffs Cachette, Clocheret, Clocheret Bas and Altiport (Arc 1600). Landings are "dans la vallée près de Bourg-Saint-Maurice". — [Bouger-Voyager, Où faire du parapente en Savoie](https://www.bouger-voyager.com/ou-faire-parapente-savoie/); [Les Arcs, vol performance](https://www.lesarcs.com/en/vol-performance-en-parapente-en-ete); [Belambra, parapente Les Arcs](https://www.belambra.fr/guides/destinations/les-arcs/parapente-les-arcs)
- **Courchevel**: tandems take off at the Col de la Loze, or from higher at La Saulire. Flights last 7–60 min "depending on wind conditions", using thermals for circuits from the Col de la Loze. Landing is at Le Praz (Courchevel 1300). — [Manawa, Courchevel Col de la Loze](https://www.manawa.com/fr-FR/activite/france/courchevel-les-trois-vallees/parapente/bapteme-de-parapente-a-courchevel-depuis-le-col-de-la-loze/9181)
- **Val d'Isère**: takeoff at the top of Solaise, about 2,500 m, overlooking Lac du Chevril and the Tignes dam. — [Airelles, paragliding Val d'Isère](https://airelles.com/en/destination/val-d-isere-hotel/experiences/paragliding-french-alps)
- Albertville sits at the confluence of the Isère and Arly, where the Tarentaise, Maurienne/Combe de Savoie and Val d'Arly meet. — [Pays d'Albertville tourism](https://www.pays-albertville.com/en/fiches/paragliding-savoie-an-rafting/) / [Wikipedia, Tarentaise Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarentaise_Valley)

### Inferences
- The breeze axis is Albertville → Moûtiers (SE), then Moûtiers → Aime → Landry → Bourg-Saint-Maurice (NE/ENE). At Landry part of the flow turns S into Peisey-Nancroix (PCHT). Above Bourg it presumably continues toward Sainte-Foy/Val d'Isère and up the Versoyen/La Rosière side to the Petit-Saint-Bernard. That last part is a deduction.
- At Moûtiers the Doron de Bozel (toward Bozel and Pralognan) and the Doron de Belleville/Allues branches take part of the flow. This is a deduction from the basin geometry; no source.

### Gaps
- I could not read the PCHT "Aérologie Hte Tarentaise" page or PDF, which very likely holds hours, strengths and venturi spots. **This is the top priority for the next pass.**
- No source on the Doron de Bozel, Belleville/Les Allues breezes, the Moûtiers confluence, or Pralognan/Vanoise aerology (Pralognan takeoffs, Mont Bochor, etc.).
- No source on La Plagne takeoffs/landings or Aime aerology. Méribel was not found.

## Q4: How do Foehn/Lombarde and synoptic winds (Bise/N, NW, W, SW/S) modify the picture, especially at the Italian border?

### Takeaway
The sources say very little. The Chamonix sheets give only site-level synoptic notes. W and N winds above 20 km/h at 2,000 m make Planpraz turbulent. Bise or light foehn ("flux descendant la vallée") makes the Prarion NE takeoff a morning site. The Plan de l'Aiguille NE side works in north wind, and its W side in a light W flow. I found **no source** on the Lombarde in Haute-Tarentaise or on Petit-Saint-Bernard exchanges.

### Cited Findings
- Planpraz: be careful in W and N winds above 20 km/h at 2,000 m (turbulent). — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Planpraz](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-de-planpraz-brevent-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5885016/)
- Prarion: a morning site with east thermals "ou flux descendant la vallée (bise ou léger foehn)" (or flow coming down the valley, in bise or light foehn). — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Prarion](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-du-prarion-les-houches-fr-5884871/)
- Plan de l'Aiguille: the NE takeoff is usable "when there is north wind". The W takeoff works in thermal/thermodynamic breeze or a light westerly flow. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Plan de l'Aiguille](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/decollage-du-plan-de-laiguille-chamonix-mont-blanc-fr-5884743/)
- "Avec un régime de NE, les brises diurnes priment sur le vent météo, protégeant le site." The site attribution (Plaine-Joux or Planpraz) is unclear; see Q1. — [FFVL PDF](https://federation.ffvl.fr/sites/ffvl.fr/files/Massifdumontblancchamonix.pdf)

### Inferences
- Down-valley flow in the Chamonix valley under N/NE (bise) or a weak S foehn makes the NE-facing morning sites (Prarion NE, Plan de l'Aiguille NE) usable. The S-facing Aiguilles Rouges launches become exposed to lee or turbulence when W/N synoptic wind exceeds about 20 km/h at 2,000 m.

### Gaps
- Lombarde (E/SE wind from Italy) at Val d'Isère, Petit-Saint-Bernard, Haute-Tarentaise and Maurienne: no source found.
- Foehn (S/SW) over Mont-Blanc and its lee effects in Chamonix and Saint-Gervais: no source found beyond the "léger foehn" mention.
- Bise effects in the Beaufortain, Megève and Tarentaise: no source.

## Q5: What is known about the other sub-sectors (Val Montjoie/Saint-Gervais, Megève/Val d'Arly, Beaufortain): takeoffs, landings, thermal triggers, soaring?

### Takeaway
Only tourism-level site data turned up: takeoff names and altitudes, landings and flight durations. There is essentially no aerology for Megève, the Beaufortain or Les Contamines.

### Cited Findings
- **Saint-Gervais**: tandems use the Bettex – Mont d'Arbois slope, taking off "face au Mont-Blanc" from the Avenaz pasture or the Mont d'Arbois (access by 4×4 or cable car). Flights last 10–15 min (balade) or 25–30 min "selon l'aérologie", with a 360° view over the Fiz, Aravis and Mont-Blanc. — [Haute-Savoie Mont-Blanc Tourisme, Vol balade biplace Saint-Gervais](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/vol-balade-biplace-saint-gervais-les-bains-fr-5829194/); [Vol rando biplace](https://hautesavoiemontblanc-tourisme.com/offres/vol-rando-biplace-saint-gervais-les-bains-fr-5831857/)

- **Megève Rochebrune**: takeoff at 1,755 m and landing on the "plaine de Praz-sur-Arly", a 755 m drop. ## Q6: What are the classic cross routes and key transitions?

### Takeaway
The sources name the classic Chamonix → Annecy route, launched from Planpraz and crossing onto the Aravis chain. They also name Planpraz → Valais (Switzerland), the Plan de l'Aiguille evening glacier run (20–30 km), and high Grands Montets flights under 4,000 m bases. Beaufortain–Tarentaise and Tarentaise–Maurienne routes were not documented.

### Cited Findings
- "Plan Praz is a perfect launch point for XCs going into the massive Wallis (Valais) valley in Switzerland, or westwards towards Annecy." A photo caption reads "crossing on to the Aravis chain on the classic XC from Chamonix to Annecy". — [XC Mag, Guide to Chamonix](https://xcmag.com/travel-guide/guide-to-chamonix-france/); [XC Mag](https://xcmag.com/?p=37799)
- Plan de l'Aiguille evening run along the massif: 20–30 km, "11 glaciers" in 2 h. — [Chamonix.net, Plan de l'Aiguille (EN)](https://www.chamonix.net/english/leisure/paragliding/plan-aiguille)
- Grands Montets: morning XC departures, and a 4,000 m cloud base on the best days. — [Chamonix.net, Grands Montets](https://www.chamonix.net/francais/loisirs/parapente/grands-montets)
- Flight over Servoz to the Chedde landing, with thermals on the Servoz/Plaine-Joux side. — [Free flying in the Mont Blanc Region (PDF)](https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/vol-libre-anglais.pdf)
- A 202 km flat triangle "de la Chartreuse aux Aravis en revenant par Albertville" (17/07/2019) shows the Albertville crossing being used in big triangles. The details were not retrieved. — [Parapente Pays de Gex](https://www.parapentepaysdegex.fr/post/le-triangle-du-17-07-19-de-la-chartreuse-aux-aravis-en-revenant-par-albertville-202-km)

### Inferences
- The Chamonix–Annecy route must cross the Arve between the Fiz/Plaine-Joux side and the Aravis, near Sallanches/Passy. The sources confirm the "crossing on to the Aravis chain" but not the exact point.

### Gaps
- Annecy → Chamonix in the reverse direction, Beaufortain → Tarentaise (e.g., Saisies → Bisanne → Grand Mont → Tarentaise), Tarentaise → Maurienne (Madeleine/Croix de Fer) and Vanoise routes: no source found. XContest flight analysis was not possible.
